01

The research, and what it found

Three findings from studying Hästens, Vispring, Savoir, Duxiana, Simba, Emma, Eight Sleep and the 2026 furniture design landscape. Each one closes a door or opens one.

Finding one - the category competes on heritage, and we cannot

Hästens founded 1852. Vispring 1901, and made the mattresses for the Titanic. Savoir created for the Savoy. Every luxury bed brand's positioning rests on the same claim: we have been doing this for a century.

We have no heritage and cannot manufacture it. Any attempt to imply age, tradition or lineage reads as pastiche and gets caught by a buyer who researches. This closes off the entire conventional route to luxury in this category.

Finding two - the most successful luxury bed brand differentiates on a pattern

Hästens is recognised worldwide by its blue and white check. Retailers describe it as "pretty much their visual signature" and note that the bed is "designed to be seen as much as felt." Not the horsehair. Not the spring count. The check.

The lesson is not to copy a pattern. It is that in a category where every product looks identical under a sheet, an ownable visual device does more work than any specification - and almost nobody in premium DTC uses one.

Finding three - warm minimalism is now the default, not a differentiator

The 2026 design press is consistent: the decade-long dominance of Scandinavian-inflected minimalism is giving way to material intensity, richer palettes and visible evidence of the maker's hand. One editor put it plainly - this is not minimalism; minimalism stripped away. What is happening now adds back, but with discrimination.

Off-white, stone, restraint, whitespace: that is what every premium DTC brand looks like now. Being more restrained is not differentiation. It is camouflage.

The Line

Our mattress has a zip running its full perimeter, dividing the white quilted top from the dark base. It is the visible expression of the one thing no competitor offers. It is also a single, clean, horizontal division between two materials.

That becomes the brand device - product-derived, instantly recognisable, and impossible to copy without copying the product.

Dark below

A true division between two grounds. Not a rule floating on one background.

The contrast principle

Light above, dark below. This is the product, and it is now the layout logic. Pages move between light and dark grounds, divided by The Line. That structural rhythm is more distinctive than any arrangement of neutrals - and it is derived from the object rather than applied to it.

06

Layout

This section is on a dark ground. The one before it was light. That alternation, divided by The Line, is the structural signature.

Grid

  • Desktop - 12 columns, 24px gutters, 1240px max, 80px page margin
  • Mobile - 4 columns, 16px gutters, 20px margin
  • Section rhythm - 128px desktop, 72px mobile

Spacing scale

  • 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24
  • 32 · 48 · 64 · 96
  • 128 · 192
  • Nothing outside this scale. Arbitrary values are the fastest route to a layout that looks amateur.

The alternating ground principle

Roughly every third section moves to the opposite ground. Not every section - the rhythm of light, light, dark is what makes structure feel composed rather than scrolled. This replaces whitespace as the primary spatial signal and is what separates our layout from the sea of all-cream premium DTC sites.

Material intensity, not flat minimalism

  • Surfaces may carry a very low-opacity texture - paper grain, a woven suggestion. Never a visible pattern.
  • Photography full-bleed at section transitions.
  • Space is generous but purposeful. Composed, not sparse.

09

Voice

Exact, calm, unsentimental. We state facts and let them work. We never reach for an adjective where a number will serve.

The heritage counter

When the category says since 1852, we say what we did instead. Never disparage the incumbents - they make fine beds. Simply be specific about the present. Specificity beats nostalgia when the buyer is comparing.

Headline formulas

FormulaExample
ContrastChange your comfort, not your mattress.
Flat statementMade to order. Made to last.
Specification as line37cm. Seven zones. One bed, for decades.
Counter-heritageWe did not inherit this. We engineered it.
Words we use
  • engineered · exact · made to order
  • refresh · replace the layer
  • seven zones · 37cm · eight to ten weeks
Words we avoid
  • heritage · timeless · artisanal
  • luxury (as an adjective)
  • revolutionary · hurry · unbeatable

Conventions

  • Sentence case everywhere except the wordmark. No exclamation marks, ever.
  • Numerals for all measurements and prices. Tabular figures.
  • Hyphens - never em or en dashes.
  • British English. Second person. Say the thing plainly.

Rules

  • Reveal is opacity 0→1 with translateY 20px→0. Trigger at 20% in viewport. Once only, never on scroll up.
  • Stagger siblings by 50ms maximum. One movement per view.
  • Nothing bounces, springs or overshoots. This brand is exact.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion - disable reveals and autoplay entirely.
  • Video muted always, captioned where speech occurs, poster frame on every instance.

12

Quality floor

Nothing ships until every line is true.

01Does it look like it came from a brand that engineers rather than inherits? If it looks heritage, it is wrong.
02Is The Line present - once, horizontal, a true division between two grounds?
03Is the contrast high? If the image has been warmed into beige it is the default aesthetic, and it is wrong.
04One typeface family. Weight and scale doing the work. Tabular figures on every number.
05Product matches reality exactly - 37cm, white quilted top, dark side panel, piping, handles.
06Contrast ratios pass AA. Nothing relies on colour alone. Focus states visible.
07Sentence case. No exclamation marks. Hyphens. Numerals for measurements.
08Motion horizontal, single, respects reduced-motion.
09No unearned badges, awards, reviews or heritage implications.
10A human has approved it.
