# Numa — Tone of Voice

The brand is stated, never argued. Everything below follows from that.

---

## The outcome

**A bed that becomes yours, and stays that way.**

Three statements carry it. Each is complete on its own and none of them mentions anybody else.

> **You do not choose the feel. You discover it.**
> Sleep on it for a year. Tell us how it should feel. We adjust it until it is.

> **It arrives finished.**
> Concierged. Room of your choice. Unrolled onto your bed. The old one leaves with it.

> **It stays right for twenty five years.**
> The part that softens renews. The part that lasts, lasts.

The word doing the work is **discover**. It removes the decision the customer is not qualified to make and replaces it with a year of finding out. That is the outcome, and it is the reason nothing else needs to be said.

---

## The first rule

**Never mention anyone else.**

No competitors, no category, no "unlike", no "most brands", no "the industry". No comparison tables. No "we do this, they do that."

If the thing is genuinely unavailable elsewhere, describing it plainly is enough. The reader works out the rest. The moment you point out that nobody else does it, you sound like you need them to notice.

**Wrong:** Most mattresses are sealed. Ours unzips.
**Right:** The part that softens renews.

**Wrong:** Unlike other brands, we include unpacking.
**Right:** It arrives finished.

---

## The second rule

**Never explain why you are good.**

State what happens. Do not justify it, defend it, or pre-empt an objection nobody raised.

**Wrong:** We pay for removal because we think a company that sells beds should take responsibility for the old one.
**Right:** The old one leaves with them.

Reasons belong on the pages built for people who go looking. Not in a hero, not in a headline, not in a product description.

---

## The word "free"

**Never used.** Not on the site, not in email, not in ads, not in support replies.

| Never | Use |
|---|---|
| Free | Complimentary, or included |
| Free of charge | With our compliments |
| At no charge | Included |
| £0.00 on a basket line | **Complimentary** |
| Free delivery | Concierge unpacking, included |

**Prefer "included" where the thing is standard.** It is the more confident word because it does not draw attention to a gift being made. Reserve *complimentary* for something given in response to a situation — the comfort layer, the mattress removal.

**Better still, omit money entirely.** "It arrives finished" beats any sentence containing a price.

---

## Named services

Three services carry names and are always capitalised.

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Concierge unpacking** | Concierged. Room of your choice. Unboxed, unrolled, positioned. Included with every mattress |
| **Adjust to Desire** | The comfort layer service. Tell us how it should feel and we adjust it until it is, with our compliments. Worth £299 |
| **Old mattress removal and recycling** | Collected on the same visit. Complimentary |

**Adjust to Desire is a tailoring service, not a warranty claim.** The verb is *adjust*. Layers stay with you.

**Right:** We send the layer that takes you there, and you keep it, with our compliments.
**Wrong:** We will make you a new comfort layer to your preference.
**Wrong:** We will send you a free replacement comfort layer.

Same offer. One is bespoke, the other is a returns department.

---

## Rhythm

**One idea per fragment. Full stops, not commas.**

> Concierged. Room of your choice. Unrolled onto your bed. The old one leaves with it.

Four beats. Each one complete. The full stops do the work that a longer sentence would have to explain.

The failure mode is a phrase pretending to be a rhythm:

**Wrong:** Concierged to the room of your choice, unboxed, unrolled onto your bed and positioned, with the comfort layer checked and seated.
**Right:** Concierged. Room of your choice. Unboxed, unrolled, positioned.

The second says less and lands harder. If a sentence has more than one comma, it is probably two sentences.

**Cut every clause that explains the previous one.** "It settles evenly and reaches full depth faster" - the second half is the first half again. Say it once.

**Never justify a benefit inside the sentence that states it.** "Not a twelve hour wait in the house" is defending against something nobody said. "A window agreed in advance" is the whole point.

Fragments are permitted and usually better. Rhythm matters more than grammar.

**Numbers written out in body copy, figures in specifications.** "Twenty five years" reads as a promise. "25 years" reads as a warranty document. Use figures in tables, dimensions and prices.

**No hedging.** Not "we believe", "we think", "we aim to". If it is true, state it. If it is not confirmed, do not write it yet.

---

## Never

- Exclamation marks
- "Just", "simply", "easy" — they imply the customer might have struggled
- Title Case In Buttons And Headings. Sentence case only
- Countdown timers, scarcity, "hurry", "while stocks last"
- "Only £2,999"
- Emoji, anywhere, in any context
- Rhetorical questions in headlines
- "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "unique", "premium" describing ourselves
- Apologising in marketing copy. Apologise in support, never in a headline

---

## Hierarchy

**Product, service, responsibility. Never inverted.**

The recycling and sustainability story is real and it is not a headline. It appears once, factually, where it is operationally relevant, and in full on the page built for people who want it.

| Placement | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Hero, banner, opening line | Never |
| In-page, where relevant | One line, factual |
| Dedicated page | Full detail |
| Post-purchase email | Prominent. They have bought. Now it is a reason to tell someone |
| PR | Lead with it here, and only here |

"The old one leaves with them" is the correct amount. Adding "for recycling" pulls the reader out of their bedroom and into logistics.

---

## Describe the service, not the staffing

Delivery is described from the customer's side of the door. How many people are involved is operational detail from the supplier's point of view, and stating it puts strangers in the reader's bedroom.

**Wrong:** Two people bring it to the room you choose.
**Right:** Concierged. Room of your choice.

The passive is correct here. It removes the people and leaves the service. Concierge already implies that somebody does it, so saying so is redundant and slightly unsettling.

The same applies to "the crew", "our team", "our drivers" and any count of anything. The mattress arrives, is unrolled, is positioned. The packaging leaves with it.

## Support and service copy

The same voice, warmer. This is the one place where apology is allowed, and it should be direct.

**Right:** Sorry it did not work out. We will collect it on Thursday.
**Wrong:** We're so sorry to hear that! 😔 Let's get this sorted for you!

Reply-to is a monitored human inbox. If the copy says a person reads it, a person reads it.

---

## The test

Read it aloud. If it sounds like it is trying to convince someone, cut it.

Everything true about this product is more persuasive stated flatly than argued. The restraint is the brand.
