1. What this is and how to use it
This is the practical guide for anyone producing visual assets — photography, AI-generated imagery, video edits and ad creative. Read Sections 3 (the product) and 4 (the integrity rule) before generating anything: they are the two places where a mistake costs real money or credibility.
The single test for anything you make
Would this look at home next to Aesop, Apple, or a boutique hotel's website? If yes, it is on-brand. If it looks like a mass-market mattress retailer's sale banner, start again. Restraint wins.
2. The brand in one page
What we sell: a premium 35cm hybrid mattress with a replaceable comfort top, sold direct, made to order, at AED 8,999–14,999 / £1,999–2,999.
The big idea: “Change your comfort, not your mattress.” The top unzips and swaps, so firmness can change over time and the bed is refreshed rather than replaced.
Brand personality: confident, calm, expert, quietly premium. Never shouty, never salesy, never cluttered.
Who we are talking to: affluent buyers in the UAE first (Western expats, affluent Indian, Emirati/Arab, and newly-arrived wealth), then the UK. Assume a discerning adult who researches before spending £2,000.
Why restraint matters commercially
At this price the buyer is comparing us to established premium brands. Busy, over-designed, discount-looking creative signals “cheap” faster than any words can, and directly reduces conversion. Space and calm signal expensive.

4. The integrity rule — non-negotiable
Real product, generated environment
The mattress must always be the REAL product. The room around it may be generated. Placing a real mattress cutout into a generated bedroom is standard, honest practice. Generating or altering the product itself — changing its depth, colour, stitching, quilting, piping or handles — is misrepresentation. It is a legal risk in both the UK and UAE, and it destroys the trust the whole business depends on.
If a generated image has changed the product, discard it. Do not “fix it in post”. Regenerate.
The unzip-and-swap must be real footage. No AI tool can invent a mechanism it has never seen. That sequence is filmed, always.
Also never
Never imply a claim the product cannot support — no frost, ice or visible “cooling” effects; no medical, postural or health claims implied by imagery.
Never include competitor products, branding, factory equipment, plastic wrapping, tools or bystanders in any frame.
Never add fake awards, “as seen in” logos, review stars or certifications we have not earned.
5. Colour
A restrained palette: one deep anchor, one warm metallic accent, quiet neutrals. Few colours, used confidently.

Off-White#F7F5F1
Soft Stone#EAE6DF

Rules
Backgrounds: mostly off-white and stone — light, airy, premium. Deep navy used sparingly for contrast.
Gold is an accent only — fine lines, small highlights, the wordmark. Never large gold fills; they read cheap.
Text: ink or navy on light; off-white on dark. Keep contrast high for readability.
Never: bright saturated colours, multiple accents, gradients, or colour used decoratively.
One exception worth knowing: the yellow foam layer inside the mattress is a real product feature and a genuine asset — it instantly communicates “separate layers that come out”. Keep it, do not desaturate it away.

8. Imagery style
Principles
Restraint and space — clean backgrounds, generous negative space, one subject in focus.
Natural, soft light — warm morning light or soft diffused studio light. No harsh flash, no busy scenes.
Tactility — show texture: the knit of the cover, the zip, the stitching, the layers. Texture sells premium materials.
Calm — nothing frantic, nothing cluttered, no visual noise.
Do
Aspirational but believable interiors — premium apartment or villa bedrooms, uncluttered, beautifully lit.
Macro detail shots of materials and construction.
The unzip-and-swap as the signature action — this is our most distinctive asset.
Leave clear space in the frame where a headline will sit.
Do not
Busy showroom or retail mattress photography — that is precisely the tier we are displacing.
Heavy text overlays, starbursts, price flashes, countdowns or sale badges.
Generic stock clichés — couples bouncing on beds, people in pyjamas laughing, thumbs-up.
Over-filtering, heavy grading, or unrealistic colour.

10. AI generation rules
Prefix every prompt with this: “Preserve the mattress exactly as provided — do not alter its shape, proportions, depth, colour, stitching, quilting pattern, piping or handles. Place it unchanged into the scene described.”
Generate in the target aspect ratio natively. Do not generate everything portrait and crop later — cropping destroys the composition the prompt created.
Generate at the tool's maximum resolution, then upscale to the final size. Current outputs at 896 x 1200 are roughly a third of what the site hero needs.
Note that 3:4 is not the same as 4:5. Meta feed needs 4:5 set explicitly.
Request two variants per scene, select the strongest, and upscale only the selected final.
Frame loose — the same scene gets re-cropped to 16:9, 4:5, 1:1 and 9:16.
Reference standard
The floating product shot on a pale grey background is the register to hold — restrained, tactile, premium, vast negative space. Dark night-time city scenes are off-palette and read closer to a US mass-premium brand; use sparingly or regenerate in daylight with the lighter palette.

12. Check before you deliver
The product matches the real mattress exactly — 35cm depth, white quilted top, dark side panel, piping, handles.
No competitor products, factory equipment, wrapping, tools, phones or bystanders in frame.
No implied claims the product cannot support; no unearned badges, awards or review stars.
Palette and lighting are consistent with the rest of the set — one coherent brand world.
Generated in the correct aspect ratio, not cropped from another shape.
Every asset has its 9:16 version for vertical placements.
Correct file format, under the size limits, sRGB, named to convention.
Both master and web-sized versions supplied.
