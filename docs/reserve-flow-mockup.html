Price and lead time must not share a viewport.

The current build shows selection, price, guarantees, delivery window and full order terms in one panel. A drop-off there tells us something went wrong but not what. Splitting the reveal is what makes the test readable.

Stage A answers: will they proceed at this price?

Stage B answers: will they still proceed knowing it takes 8 to 10 weeks?

STAGE B

Lead time and terms

Revealed only after Continue. Intent is now established, so the wait is disclosed to someone who has already decided on price. A drop-off here is a lead-time rejection and nothing else.

Currently on the site
  • Price, guarantees, delivery window and full order terms in one panel
  • Roughly 120 words before the button
  • A drop-off is uninterpretable
  • Terms compete with the price for attention
Proposed
  • Stage A is a basket. Under 20 words
  • Stage B carries the wait and the terms
  • Two clean measurement points
  • Lead time still discoverable in FAQ, delivery page and footer

This is progressive disclosure, not concealment. The delivery window stays in the FAQ, on the delivery page and in the footer throughout. It simply does not share an eyeline with the price. Revealing it once intent exists is cleaner test design and better selling.

NOTES

Three things to decide

Prices have moved

The screenshot shows £1,999 / £2,499 / £2,999 / £3,499. The brochure and financial model carry £1,549 / £1,999 / £2,499 / £2,899. Both need updating to whichever is final - the model's contribution figures shift with it.

Super King marked unavailable

It is the highest-value size and it is switched off. Given a 40-unit MOQ applies whichever size is ordered, consider making it orderable on a stated longer lead time rather than turning the revenue away. If it stays off, remove the price - showing a price for something nobody can buy is friction without purpose.

The reassurance line in Stage A

"Cancel any time before dispatch" reverses risk without revealing the wait. It lifts intent uniformly across price variants, so it does not contaminate the price read. Keep it to one line - a paragraph here reintroduces the problem the split was built to solve.
