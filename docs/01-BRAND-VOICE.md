# Brand voice

The single governing voice document. Supersedes `numa-tone-of-voice.md`, `13-BRAND-VOICE.md`, and the voice sections of `brand-guidelines.html`, `10-BRAND-GUIDELINES-DEV.md` and `6-COPY-SPEC.md`.

Contains wording rules only. Anything that could change when a supplier answers an email or a partner signs a contract lives in `02-FACTS-AND-CLAIMS.md`, not here.

---

## 1. The overriding rule

**Clarity is never traded for voice.** Both are achievable in the same line.

If a sentence is on-voice but makes a first-time reader stop and work out what it means, it is not finished. Compression is the style, not the goal. A line that has to be decoded has failed, however well it reads to someone who already knows the product.

Every other rule in this document is subordinate to this one.

---

## 2. The voice in one line

**Exact, calm, unsentimental.** State facts and let them work. Never reach for an adjective where a number will serve, and never explain a benefit the reader has already understood.

The brand is stated, never argued.

---

## 3. Where it comes from

Every competitor in premium sleep sells heritage, and theirs is earned. We have none and will not pretend to. So the voice does the opposite job: it establishes credibility through precision rather than lineage.

That is why the voice is spare. Not cold, spare. The difference lives in whether the sentence is useful.

---

## 4. The four rules

### 4.1 Say the outcome. Stop.
If a sentence explains why the outcome happens, cut it. The specification panel exists for that.

| No | Yes |
|---|---|
| A 400g cool-knit cover over gel memory foam and a wave-profile layer that channels air through the mattress rather than trapping it. | Air moves through it instead of sitting under you. |

### 4.2 Short beats correct, until it stops being clear.
A technically fuller sentence nobody finishes is worse than a shorter one they do. But a shorter one they have to decode is worse than both. Rule 1 wins.

| No | Yes |
|---|---|
| Every spring is individually pocketed, so movement stays where it happens. If your partner gets up at five, reads late, or turns over twenty times a night, it does not travel across the bed to you. | Movement stays on their side of the bed. |

### 4.3 No connective tissue.
`Which is why`, `so that`, `meaning that` are all signs the sentence is doing two jobs. Split it or cut it.

### 4.4 Never mention anyone else.
No competitors, no category, no `unlike`, no `most brands`, no `the industry`. No comparison tables. Never compare price to anyone else's, by name or by range.

If the thing is genuinely unavailable elsewhere, describing it plainly is enough. The moment you point out that nobody else does it, you sound like you need them to notice.

| No | Yes |
|---|---|
| Most mattresses are sealed. Ours unzips. | The part that softens is replaceable. |
| Unlike other brands, we include unpacking. | It arrives finished. |

---

## 5. Conventions

| Rule |
|---|
| Sentence case everywhere except the wordmark |
| No exclamation marks. Ever |
| **Numbers render as figures. One rule, no exceptions by context** |
| Hyphens only. No em dashes, no en dashes |
| British English. Second person |
| Say `do not`, not `don't` |
| Brand name comes from a setting. Never hard-coded |
| No emoji, anywhere, in any context |
| No rhetorical questions in headlines |
| No hedging. Not `we believe`, `we think`, `we aim to`. If it is true, state it. If it is not confirmed, do not write it yet |

---

## 6. Rhythm

**One idea per fragment. Full stops, not commas.**

> Concierge unpacking. Room of your choice. Unrolled onto your bed.

Three beats, each complete. The full stops do the work a longer sentence would have to explain.

The failure mode is a phrase pretending to be a rhythm:

| No | Yes |
|---|---|
| Concierged to the room of your choice, unboxed, unrolled onto your bed and positioned, with the comfort layer checked and seated. | Concierge unpacking. Room of your choice. Unboxed, unrolled, positioned. |

If a sentence has more than one comma, it is probably two sentences.

**Cut every clause that explains the previous one.** `It settles evenly and reaches full depth faster` says the same thing twice.

**Never justify a benefit inside the sentence that states it.** `Not a twelve hour wait in the house` defends against something nobody said.

Fragments are permitted and usually better. Rhythm matters more than grammar, and clarity matters more than rhythm.

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|

| Flat statement | Made to order. Made to last. |
| Specification as a line | 35cm. Five feels. One bed. |
| Counter-heritage | We did not inherit this. We engineered it. |

## 8. Never write

### 8.1 Words

| Word | Why |
|---|---|
| `free` | See section 9. Never, in any context |
| `premium`, `luxury` as an adjective | If it needs saying, it is not |
| `heritage`, `timeless`, `artisanal` | Borrowed from brands that earned it |
| `hotel-grade` | Borrowed authority |
| `engineered` as decoration | Fine in `engineered core`. Meaningless in `engineered support` |
| `designed for`, `built for` | States intent, not outcome. Say what it does |
| `just`, `simply`, `easy` | Implies the customer might have struggled |
| `revolutionary`, `game-changing`, `unique`, `unbeatable`, `hurry` | Nobody believes it |
| `which is why`, `so that`, `meaning that` | The sentence is doing two jobs |
| `perfect for everyone` | Nothing is |
| Title Case In Headings And Buttons | Sentence case only |
| Countdowns, stock counters, scarcity, `while stocks last` | Wrong at this price |
| `Only £2,999` | Never frame the price as small |

### 8.2 Defensive writing

**Never apologise in marketing copy.** The honesty shows in what we do not claim, not in announcing that we are not claiming it. Never explain why we are good. State what happens, and do not pre-empt an objection nobody raised.

| No | Yes |
|---|---|
| made for warm nights without overclaiming miracles | Heat is why people give up on a mattress they used to love. This one lets it out. |
| We pay for removal because we think a company that sells beds should take responsibility for the old one. | Your old mattress leaves with the packaging. |
| Most brands do not publish this | Publish it and let them notice |

Reasons belong on the pages built for people who go looking. Not in a hero, not in a headline, not in a product description.

### 8.3 Substantiation

**Never quantify a performance claim without evidence.** Construction may be described. Performance may not be quantified. This rule holds permanently, whatever the current facts are; what is currently substantiated is recorded in `02-FACTS-AND-CLAIMS.md`.

Never state a certification without its certificate number displayed.

Environmental claims are performance claims. `Recycled`, `recycling`, `sustainable` and `carbon` describe what physically happens and must be evidenced before use.

## 9. The word `free`

**Never used.** Not on the site, not in email, not in ads, not in support replies.

| Never | Use |
|---|---|
| Free | Complimentary, or included |
| Free of charge | With our compliments |
| At no charge | Included |
| £0.00 on a basket line | **Complimentary** |
| Free delivery | Delivery included |

**If we are not obliged to offer it, it is complimentary. If the category expects it, it is included.**

Delivery is the only thing described as included, because the mattress has to arrive. Everything else we could decline to offer, so everything else is complimentary.

**Better still, omit money entirely.** `It arrives finished` beats any sentence containing a price.

---

## 10. Named services

Service names are always capitalised.

**A service name never appears alone on first use.** A name that describes itself can stand alone. A name that does not carries its benefit on every first appearance, because a name means nothing to a first-time reader in isolation.

| Name | Self-describing | First-use rendering |
|---|---|---|
| **Concierge unpacking** | Yes | Stands alone |
| **Old mattress removal** | Yes | Stands alone |
| **Adjust to Desire** | No | **Adjust to Desire.** A year to get the feel right. |

**Adjust to Desire is a tailoring service, not a warranty claim.** The verb is *adjust*. Layers stay with the customer.

| No | Yes |
|---|---|
| We will make you a new comfort layer to your preference. | We send the layer that takes you there, and you keep it, with our compliments. |
| We will send you a free replacement comfort layer. | Same as above |

Same offer. One is bespoke, the other is a returns department.

---

## 11. Time periods

Three periods exist and they are separate things. **They must never share phrasing or units.**

| Period | Language | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| First 14 days | No charge if you change your mind | Days |
| The return window | 100 nights to return it | Nights |
| The adjustment year | A year to get the feel right | Years |

**The adjustment year is never counted in nights.** Not `365 nights`, not a `365 night comfort programme`. Two periods measured in the same unit is what makes customers unable to tell them apart, and it is the failure mode this brand is most prone to.

---

## 12. Two actions, not preferences

The flip is described as **turning a layer over.** Never as opening, accessing, or going inside anything.

| Action | Words |
|---|---|
| Flip, to change the firmness | Unzip. Turn over. Zip up. |
| Swap, to replace when worn | Unzip. Lift out. Swap in. |

Same zip, different reason. Blurring them makes changing firmness sound like maintenance.

---

## 13. Describe the service, not the staffing

Delivery is described from the customer's side of the door. How many people are involved is operational detail, and stating it puts strangers in the reader's bedroom.

| No | Yes |
|---|---|
| Two people bring it to the room you choose. | Concierge unpacking. Room of your choice. |

The same applies to `the crew`, `our team`, `our drivers` and any count of anyone.

This constrains wording. It does not protect an unclear line from being rewritten: `Concierged` used as a verb was on-voice and still failed rule 1.

---

## 14. Where things lead

| Where | What leads |
|---|---|
| Hero | What it does for you |
| Benefits blocks | The outcome, in plain language |
| Section copy | The experience, then how it works |
| Specification panel | The number, with a short translation |
| FAQ | Whatever the question asks |

**A number appears above the specification panel only when it is the point.** 35cm is a size. 100 nights is a promise. Densities, gsm and wire gauges are not.

**Product, service, responsibility. Never inverted.** The recycling and sustainability story is real and it is not a headline.

| Placement | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Hero, banner, opening line | Never. The hero rhythm already carries three ideas |
| In-page, where relevant | One line, factual |
| Dedicated page | Full detail |
| Post-purchase email | Prominent. They have bought. Now it is a reason to tell someone |
| PR | Lead with it here, and only here |

---

## 15. Support and service copy

The same voice, warmer. This is the one place apology is allowed, and it should be direct.

| No | Yes |
|---|---|
| We're so sorry to hear that! Let's get this sorted for you! | Sorry it did not work out. We will collect it on Thursday. |

Reply-to is a monitored human inbox. If the copy says a person reads it, a person reads it.

---

## 16. The test for any sentence

1. Would a first-time reader understand it immediately?
2. Would a person say this out loud?
3. Is it doing one job or two?
4. Does it explain something the reader already understood?
5. Is every claim in it substantiated?
6. Could it be shorter and still clear?

If a sentence fails any of these, it is not finished. Question 1 outranks the rest.
