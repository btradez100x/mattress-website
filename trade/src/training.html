Training

Handling the objection, not the person.

Every objection below is one a buyer has a right to raise. The answer is never to argue with it.

How to use this

3 rules before the table

Concede first

The strongest answer grants what the buyer already knows and pivots to what they do not. Arguing with a true objection loses the room.

Answer on their economics

Almost every price objection dissolves when the number is reframed against what the premises earn. Unit price is our frame, not theirs.

Never invent a concession

If the answer is not here, say you will come back with it. A promise you cannot keep costs more than a slow answer.

Objection handling

15, and the answer to each

Kept current. If you hit one that is not here, send it to us and it gets added.

The objectionWhat is really being askedHow to answer it
"It is far too expensive."They are comparing against a contract mattress budget, which is the wrong comparison.Concede: it is more than a contract mattress, and that is deliberate. Pivot: over 7 years at normal occupancy it is about a pound per occupied room night, and the alternative is about 20 pence. The difference is 79p a night against a room rate of hundreds. Ask what they charge, then do the sum in front of them.
"We can get mattresses for 400 pounds."True, and they are not buying the same thing.Concede: they can, and for a budget property that is the right decision. Pivot: ask what the bed contributes to their review scores. If sleep quality appears in their guest feedback, the 400 pound bed is the reason. Do not disparage the alternative.
"We have never heard of you."A fair objection from a buyer committing 100 rooms.Concede: we are new to hospitality and there is no long reference list. Pivot: hand over the compliance pack unasked. Fire certification, insurance, company standing, modern slavery statement, specification sheet. Most new suppliers cannot produce that in the first meeting.
"Our brand standard specifies a different bed."This is a hard stop, not an objection.Do not try to work around it. Ask whether the property is flagged and whether the bed is brand-specified. If it is, log the contact and move on. Substitutions need formal brand approval and failing that causes a failed inspection.
"Can you do better on price?"The default question. It is a test as often as a request.Concede: everyone asks, and it is the right question to ask. Pivot: prices are published and fixed, which is what stops another buyer getting a better number than them. Then offer the one real lever: 3 percent for payment in full at order, on the bands where it is available.
"We need 60 day terms."Standard in FF&E and it will come up on most contract deals.Concede: that is normal in the industry. Pivot: every bed is made to order, so the deposit is what funds the build rather than pricing risk into every unit. Quote the published terms. Anything below the standard deposit comes back to us before you answer it.
"Why do you need a deposit at all?"They are used to buying from stock.Nothing is sold from a shelf. The deposit is what starts production. Without it there is no build, and a date cannot be quoted. Framed that way it is a schedule question rather than a credit question.
"We want to hold 10 percent until snagging."Normal practice, and reasonable.Agree it. Do not resist. Agree the release window in writing at quote stage rather than at delivery. Roll-packed beds have very little to snag, so the risk is low and the concession buys goodwill.
"Your lead time is too long."Sometimes true, and if it is, say so.Ask the handover date at the first call rather than at the quote. If the programme does not fit, say so immediately. A missed date costs the reference, which on a contract sale is worth more than the order.
"What if the guests do not like them?"The real objection behind most hesitation.This is the strongest card in the deck. 1 change per bed within 12 months of delivery, a replacement comfort layer in a different feel, for every bed in the order. They keep both. The decision stops being permanent.
"Can we trial 5 first?"Reasonable, and it does not fit the minimums.Concede: wanting to feel it before committing a floor is exactly right. Pivot: offer a sample unit rather than a trial order. The minimum exists because every bed is made to order, and a 5 unit build cannot be produced.
"We only need 10 rooms in our own fire spec."Below the bespoke minimum.Say no, and say why. 25 per size on a bespoke build. Offer the standard specification instead if the premises allow it, or ask whether the order can be brought up. Do not take it and hope.
"We buy through our procurement agent."Common, and not an obstacle.Treat the agent as the customer. They buy on the owner's behalf and they are paid to. We supply agents on the same published terms and do not undercut them with the owner. Ask who is procuring at the first call so this never surprises you.
"Who else have you supplied?"The reference question, and it will keep coming until there is an answer.Do not overstate. Name what is true and move to the specification and the compliance pack. If it is a property that would make a good first reference, say so and ask what it would take.
"Send me a price list and I will come back."Usually a polite close.Send it, and attach 1 question that needs an answer. What the premises charge, or the handover date. A price list alone gives them nothing to reply to.

The 4 questions that qualify

Ask these before anything else

First

What do the premises charge?

Per night, per month or per unit. It sorts a real buyer from a tyre kicker in 1 question.

Second

Is the property flagged?

If a brand standard specifies the bed, there is no deal here yet.

Third

What is the handover date?

If the programme does not fit the lead time, say so now rather than at quote.

Fourth

Who signs the order?

The owner, the operator or a procurement agent. It changes who you are selling to.
