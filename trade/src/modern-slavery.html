Voluntary statement

Modern slavery and human trafficking.

Published voluntarily. {{brand.legal}} sits below the turnover threshold at which a statement is required, and publishes one because buyers are entitled to ask.

Scope

What this covers

This statement covers {{statement.period}} and applies to {{brand.legal}}, trading as {{brand.name}}.

Section 54 of the Modern Slavery Act 2015 requires a statement from organisations with an annual turnover of thirty six million pounds or more. {{brand.legal}} is below that threshold and has no legal obligation to publish. We publish because contract buyers ask, and because a supplier who will not answer the question is answering it.

Nothing in this statement is aspirational. Where a control is not yet in place, it says so.

Structure and supply chain

How the business is put together

The company

{{brand.legal}} is a private limited company registered in England and Wales, incorporated in {{brand.incorporated}}. It designs and sells mattresses under the {{brand.name}} name. It has no subsidiaries.

Manufacturing

A single contracted manufacturing partner produces every mattress and every comfort layer. There is no second factory and no sub-contracting arranged by us. The relationship is direct, not brokered through an agent.

Logistics

Ocean freight, customs clearance and warehousing are handled by contracted third parties. Final mile delivery in the United Kingdom is a contracted carrier.

Workforce

{{brand.legal}} has no manufacturing workforce of its own. The highest risk in this supply chain sits with the manufacturing partner and with the labour used by logistics contractors, not with our own staff.

Risk

Where the risk actually sits

Stating this honestly is more useful than stating that we take it seriously.

Manufacturing

The single largest exposure. Textile and foam manufacturing carries known risks around working hours, wage deductions, recruitment fees and agency labour.

Raw materials

Below our direct contractual relationship. Steel, foam and textile inputs are sourced by the manufacturer. Our visibility past our direct partner is limited and we do not claim otherwise.

Logistics and delivery

Freight and warehousing use sub-contracted labour. Final mile delivery in the United Kingdom is a lower risk but not a zero one.

What is in place

Controls, as they stand today

Direct relationship

We contract with the manufacturer directly rather than through a trading agent, so there is no intermediary obscuring who makes the product or under what conditions.

Supplier terms

Our purchase terms prohibit forced, bonded and child labour, prohibit recruitment fees being charged to workers, and require compliance with local labour law on hours and wages.

Single source, known site

Every unit comes from one named site. We are not placing orders across a rotating pool of factories, which is where visibility is usually lost.

Right to audit

Our terms reserve the right to audit or to appoint a third party to audit.

Escalation

Any concern raised by a member of staff, a partner or a customer is investigated by a director. There is no threshold below which a report is ignored.

What is not yet in place

Said plainly

An independent social compliance audit of the manufacturing site has not yet been commissioned. We have not completed a formal mapping of the supply chain beyond our direct manufacturing partner. We do not yet run structured modern slavery training, because the company is small enough that the people making sourcing decisions are the directors.

These are the three gaps a procurement reviewer would find, so we have listed them rather than waiting to be asked.

A statement that claims a mature programme at this size would not be credible. This one describes a small company with a direct relationship to a single manufacturer, which is a genuine advantage in visibility and a genuine limitation in independent verification.

Commitments

For the coming year

  1. Commission an independent audit

    Of the manufacturing site, by a recognised third party, with the findings summarised in next year's statement.

  2. Map to raw material tier

    Identify the principal input suppliers used by our manufacturing partner.

  3. Formalise supplier declarations

    An annual written declaration from the manufacturing partner and from each logistics contractor.

  4. Publish against these

    Next year's statement will report on each of these four, including any we have not achieved.
