The bed

20 centimetres of springs. 15 of comfort. 1 year to get it right.

A premium bed is bought on a promise the buyer cannot test. this one is built so the promise does not have to be taken on faith.

Why it matters

A bed is the only thing in the room they spend 8 hours on

Every other item in a hotel bedroom is looked at. this one is slept on. It is the single line in an FF&E budget that a guest reports on afterwards.

The review says the bed

Comfort and sleep quality appear in guest feedback more than any other room item. A bed that sleeps badly is a score that does not recover.

It is bought once a decade

Nothing else in the room carries that long a commitment on a decision made from a sample.

It costs almost nothing per night

Over 7 years at normal occupancy, a Super King is about a pound per occupied room night. Against the room rate it disappears.

{{brand.core}}

20 centimetres, 7 zones, 2 millimetre gauge

The core is the part nobody sees and everybody feels.

Individually pocketed springs, arranged in 7 zones across the length of the bed. The zones under the hips and shoulders are wound to a different gauge from the zones under the legs.

Pocketed means each spring moves alone. 1 sleeper turning over does not move the other.

Not glued. Glue is permanent, and nothing about this bed is meant to be.

Springs being wound and encased

{{brand.gel}} and {{brand.cover}}

The 15 centimetres above the core

{{brand.gel}} is gel-infused memory foam. It holds shape under pressure and releases it as the sleeper moves, rather than setting into a hollow.

{{brand.cover}} is a 400 gram cool-knit cover, quilted in vertical channels. The channels are structural as well as visual. They stop the fill migrating, which is what turns a good bed into a lumpy one after 2 years of housekeeping.

Gel in the middle does nothing. It has to be where the sleeper is.
