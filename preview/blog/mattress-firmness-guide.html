Mattress Firmness Guide: Soft, Medium and Firm Explained

12 August 2026 · Aligna

Soft, medium or firm? A clear firmness guide for real sleepers - by position, body type and what “support” actually means.

Firmness is the first thing shoppers feel - and the easiest thing brands oversell. A “firm orthopaedic” label does not guarantee comfort. What matters is whether the surface pressure and deep support match your body and sleep style.

What firmness scales mean

Most retailers use a 1-10 scale (1 very soft, 10 very firm) or plain language: soft / medium-soft / medium / medium-firm / firm. Scales are not universal across brands, so treat them as relative within one product line.

Soft mattresses

Best for: lighter side sleepers who need shoulder and hip cushioning; people who dislike “on top of the bed” feel.

Watch for: too much sink can make changing position harder and may leave the midsection unsupported for stomach or back sleepers.

Medium and medium-firm

This is the most common sweet spot for combination and back sleepers. You get contouring at the surface with a stable core underneath. If you are unsure, start here - then adjust softer or firmer based on a proper home trial.

Firm mattresses

Best for: many stomach sleepers; some heavier back sleepers who bottom out on plush foams.

Watch for: pressure points at shoulders/hips for side sleepers; “hard” is not the same as “supportive”.

Body weight changes the equation

Two people on the same mattress can report different firmness. More weight compresses comfort layers deeper. If you and a partner differ significantly in weight, look for zoned support, a balanced hybrid, or a comfort system that can be tuned (for example, different comfort layers).

Support vs cushioning

  • Cushioning reduces peak pressure where bone meets mattress.
  • Support keeps your torso from sagging relative to hips and shoulders.

You need both. Soft all the way through can feel nice for five minutes and fail at 3am. Firm all the way through can feel “correct” in a shop and leave you shifting all night.

How to choose without a showroom

  1. Name your primary sleep position.
  2. Note body weight range (yours and partner’s).
  3. Prefer constructions that state layer purpose (comfort / transition / core).
  4. Use a trial long enough to adapt - often weeks, not a single night.
If comfort needs change over time, a replaceable comfort layer can be a more rational path than discarding an entire mattress when only the surface has softened.

FAQ

Is medium-firm the safest choice?

It is a sensible default for many adults, especially combination sleepers - but side sleepers with sharp pressure points may still prefer a plush top over a supportive core.

Can a topper fix firmness?

A quality topper can soften feel; it rarely adds deep support. If the core has sagged, a topper is a temporary patch.
